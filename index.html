01

Mesopotamia

Located between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers in modern-day Iraq, Mesopotamia is often called the "cradle of civilization."

The Sumerians developed cuneiform, one of the earliest writing systems, pressed into clay tablets with a reed stylus. They also built some of the first cities with organised government, and later rulers such as Hammurabi issued written legal codes covering trade, property, and punishment — among the earliest known law codes in history.

c. 3100 BCE

Cuneiform writing develops in Sumer, one of the earliest writing systems known.

c. 1754 BCE

The Code of Hammurabi is inscribed, one of the earliest and most complete written legal codes.

02

Ancient Egypt

Centred along the Nile River, Egyptian civilization lasted for roughly three thousand years.

Egypt was ruled by pharaohs believed to hold a divine role, and is remembered for monumental architecture such as the pyramids at Giza, a system of hieroglyphic writing, and advances in medicine, astronomy, and engineering. The predictable annual flooding of the Nile supported farming that sustained one of the ancient world's largest populations.

03

Ancient Greece

Greek city-states developed contrasting political systems and left a lasting mark on philosophy, science, and government.

Athens

Credited with an early form of democracy, where free male citizens could vote directly on laws and policy.

Sparta

A rival city-state built around military discipline and a highly structured citizen-soldier society.

Philosophy

Thinkers including Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle shaped Western ideas about logic, ethics, and politics.

Mathematics

Figures such as Pythagoras and Euclid produced foundational work in geometry still taught today.

04

Ancient China

Successive dynasties shaped Chinese civilization over thousands of years.

The Qin dynasty unified China in 221 BCE and began connecting earlier defensive walls into what eventually became the Great Wall. Later dynasties, including the Han and Tang, developed papermaking, the compass, and one of the world's oldest continuous bureaucratic systems of government, staffed through competitive examinations.

Elsewhere, the Indus Valley civilization built carefully planned cities with drainage systems as early as 2500 BCE, and the Maya in Mesoamerica independently developed complex calendars, writing, and astronomy.

05

Glossary of terms

Cuneiform
One of the earliest writing systems, made of wedge-shaped marks pressed into clay.
Hieroglyphics
The formal writing system of ancient Egypt, using pictorial symbols.
City-state
An independent city that governs itself and its surrounding territory, common in ancient Greece.
Dynasty
A sequence of rulers from the same family or line, common in ancient China and Egypt.
Empire
A large political unit made up of multiple territories or peoples under one ruling power.
Ziggurat
A tiered, pyramid-shaped temple structure built in ancient Mesopotamia.
